## 3. The S1 distance discrepancy

**Facts** (`review_distance_diagnostics.json`, `review_distance_consistency.json`, `review_distance_consistency_variants.json`, `review_distance_mechanism.json`, `review_dE_bias_bound.json`; all from saved files):

1. *How distance is computed.* In both implementations distance is an independent integrated state: Modelica `Plant.mo` `der(dist) = vp` (`vp = max(v, 0)`), Python `plant.py` `dy[dist] = v`. It is not derived from the reference profile, from `v_ref` interpolation or from the knot list.
2. *Same mismatch in all cases.* The S1 end-distance difference Modelica minus Python(0.1 ms) is -8.39e-7 to -8.41e-7 m (relative -3.9e-10) in **all 8** S1 runs (fixed and scheduled, SoH 1.0, 0.9, 0.8, 0.7) and -5.0e-8 to -5.2e-8 m (relative -9.5e-11, within tolerance) in all 8 S2 runs. The corresponding chemical-energy difference is -1.5e-4 to -1.6e-4 J (relative -4.0e-10 to -4.1e-10) in S1. It therefore does not depend on the controller or on SoH.
3. *Where it accumulates.* 95.6 % of the S1 total (-8.03e-7 of -8.40e-7 m) accumulates inside the 50 km/h constant-speed segment (157 to 197 s); the next largest are the 35 km/h cruise and its acceleration (-1.9e-8 and -1.6e-8 m). Every other segment contributes less than 1.6e-8 m. Inside the 50 km/h cruise it grows quadratically in time inside the 50 km/h cruise. It does not occur at profile knots: the segment is smooth.
4. *Internal inconsistency of the Modelica state, not a Python error.* Over 160 to 195 s the change of Modelica's `dist` differs from Simpson's integral of Modelica's own saved `v` by -8.001e-7 m (Python at 1 ms, 0.1 ms and 20 microseconds: +1.1e-9, -3.0e-9, -7.0e-8 m). The chemical energy shows the same pattern (Modelica's `E_chem` change minus 48 x Simpson integral of `I` is 1.6e-4 J lower than Python's, same relative size as distance). The Python dt = 2e-5 diagnostic (earlier in the same task) moves Python's end distance by only 5.8e-8 m from 1e-4 to 2e-5 while Modelica stays 7.8e-7 m below it; Modelica's speed is within 1.4e-10 m/s of that run.
5. *Tolerance independence.* The window residual is -8.001e-7 m at DASSL tolerance 1e-12, 1e-10 and -7.988e-7 m at 1e-8, for the fixed and scheduled controller and at SoH 0.7 and 0.8: it does not respond to the solver tolerance although other quantities change by up to 100-fold between those tolerances. DASSL takes about 46 steps per 10 ms interval (1 511 591 steps for 32 492 time events in the logged run), so the earlier `-maxStepSize=0.001` test did not change step sizes materially and is **uninformative**.
6. *Sample-time drift hypothesis: partly supported, then falsified.* OpenModelica's controller update times differ from `k*Ts` by `delta_k` (up to 1.5e-10 s at the end of S1). Inside the 50 km/h window the per-sample deficit equals `v * delta_k` (correlation 1.000, ratio 13.8 to 14.1 against v = 13.89 m/s). But the relation `d4_k = -sum_j v_j delta_j` fails globally: it predicts -7.4e-6 m at the end of S1 (observed -8.4e-7, ratio 0.11) and the wrong sign for S2 (predicted +1.3e-7, observed -5.1e-8, `review_distance_mechanism.json`). The coincidence inside the window is not a mechanism.
7. *Effect on reported metrics* (end states vs the 2e-5 Python run, S1 fixed SoH 0.8): relative bias of distance -3.62e-10 and of energy -3.74e-10, so Wh/km differs by -6.2e-10 (1.2e-11 relative). Across all 16 runs the relative bias of Wh/km is between -2.6e-11 and +1.45e-10 (including the Python 1e-4 step error), and the induced bias of dE% is at most 4.3e-9 pp (S2, SoH 0.8) and 1.7e-9 pp (S1, SoH 0.9), against dE% of 2.3e-3 and 1.2e-3. Speed tracking (RMSE, max error, the controller trace) depends on `v` only; the RMSE differs from the saved value by about 1e-10 m/s and the largest speed difference against the 0.1 ms run is 2.1e-9 m/s. The preregistered labels cannot be affected (margin of 1 % against an effect of 0.005 % plus a bias of 1e-8 %).

**Classification: unresolved cause, bounded impact.** The check failed its declared tolerance and stays failed. The most that can be said is: (a) not a controller, SoH or tolerance effect; (b) not the reference profile, interpolation or knots; (c) the Modelica accumulator states are inconsistent with the Modelica speed by about 1e-9 relative during one segment; (d) the likely class of explanation is the interaction of the solver with the many time events (the drift coincidence), but no test of it was performed. A bounded way to decide it, not run here: re-simulate S1 fixed SoH 0.8 for 0 to 200 s with the same model and a logged step count, once with the controller `sample()` replaced by a drift-free time-event formulation. That changes the model formulation and needs its own dated amendment; it is not required for any reported conclusion.

## 6. Is a constrained-regime test necessary?

**Why the question is open.** The headroom in the current runs (`review_constraint_headroom.json`): peak torque request 51.2 Nm against a limit of at least 55.3 Nm (S1; ratio 0.62); S2 ratio 0.38; braking request at most 0.53 of the negative limit; no friction-brake force; peak electrical power 12.8 kW (S1) against a battery capability `(V_oc - V1)^2/(4 R0)` of 115 kW at SoH 1.0 and 100 kW at SoH 0.7 (peak ratio 0.11 to 0.13); minimum terminal voltage 46.35 V at SoH 0.7 (96.6 % of V_oc). Resistive losses are 1.1 to 2.1 % of the supplied energy, which bounds how much a controller could change the energy through resistance in the unconstrained regime.

**The physical link in the current model.** SoH enters only through `R0(SoH)`, `R1(SoH)` (coefficients `k_r0 = 0.5`, `k_r1 = 0.3`, both `report_unverified`) and the capacity `Q_Ah * SoH` in the state-of-charge equation. The torque and power limits (`T_max = 85 N m`, `P_max = 18 kW`) are constants independent of SoH and of DC-bus voltage; the model has no minimum terminal voltage, no discharge or charge current limit and no power derating. Therefore, in this model, SoH **cannot** change whether a constraint is active, and a constrained scenario with the same limits would apply them identically to the fixed and scheduled controller. A test in which SoH matters through constraints would test a coupling that I would have to assume.

**Parameters needed for a physically meaningful SoH-to-constraint link** (none is available in the project as a sourced value):

| Quantity | Unit | Provenance needed | Effect on the conclusion if wrong/missing |
|---|---|---|---|
| minimum allowed terminal voltage (cut-off) | V | pack/BMS datasheet | defines the voltage-limited power `P = V_min (V_oc - V_min)/R0`; without it no voltage-driven limit exists |
| maximum discharge current (continuous and peak, with duration), and its SoH dependence | A | pack/BMS datasheet or test | sets the SoH-dependent power ceiling |
| maximum charge current / maximum charge voltage (regeneration acceptance) | A, V | pack/BMS datasheet | determines whether regeneration is clipped, which changes energy per km |
| `R0(SoH)`, `R1(SoH)` as measured functions (also temperature) | ohm | measurement or cell-level literature | replaces unverified `k_r0`, `k_r1`; the whole energy effect scales with them |
| `V_oc(SoC, SoH)` | V | measurement | constant 48 V is assumed; sag and available power depend on it |
| motor/inverter torque and power limits as functions of DC-bus voltage | N m, W | motor/inverter datasheet | the only route by which a sagging bus can reduce `T_lim` in the model |
| thermal derating | W/K | datasheet | optional |
| whether the controller is told the derated limit | (design decision) | project decision | decides whether anti-windup can be SoH-aware |

**Recommendation.** Defer the claim that SoH scheduling does or does not matter when constraints are active, pending external parameter evidence. A clearly labelled assumption-based exploratory simulation is possible but only as sensitivity analysis over declared parameter ranges (not a point value tuned to make a limit bind), and it must not be presented as evidence about a real battery. It is not scientifically justified as a primary experiment today.

**Prerequisite for any such experiment (not started).** A dated protocol amendment, written before the runs, defining: the scenarios (e.g. a profile whose demanded power exceeds the sourced ceiling at SoH 0.7), the controller comparison (fixed, scheduled, and whether the scheduled controller sees the derated limit), the success/failure criteria and metrics (energy per km with the same definition, RMSE, time at the limit, anti-windup activity, friction-brake energy), the numerical-treatment table of section 4.4 applied to every case, and the status of each parameter (sourced / assumed range). Without it no run should start.
